1 Purpose and source authority
This References Master turns the Hapa Versus specification and visual direction into a practical working reference for designers, artists and coding agents. Its recommendation is to begin with a small deterministic game core, present its accepted outcomes through the pinned Lumina fork, and prove the Card and territory loop with two independently validating peers. The visual goal is a tangible brushed-pixel battlefield in which a player can trace every important change to its source.
The book combines an implementation cheat sheet, selected repositories and production tools, independent acceptance examples, a new twelve-image visual atlas, and a staged agent handoff. It is the third member of the design set. Read the quick start before choosing dependencies; use the atlas while building presentation; use the source register when checking a factual claim or revisiting a tool choice.

The repository guide gives each resource a job and an adoption status. Current Pear packaging and OpenAI agent-workflow references were checked again rather than copied from older tutorials. Preserve the decisions already made
The current ten-row starting field has nine columns and four deployment rows in total. Its full frame is 11 by 14 after banks are included. Formations are actual member placements, with defender-relative incoming faces; the early shorthand percentages are not the implemented formation rules. Minor Arcana occupy nine public column slots per player, and replacement differs from the expiry of burned Mana. Major Arcana remain in their bank but follow the target rule written on the Card, which may bind a traveling Mount. Public plan agreement determines when resolution may begin. [REQ03 chapters 3–10 and 14; Appendix A]
These points are especially easy for an agent to lose when working from an attractive image or a familiar reference game. The implementation should explain the selected Hapa behavior even when that differs from the agent's memory of Chess, Magic or a typical real-time tactics engine.

A useful first working demonstration
Run the named ten-row opening fixture. Show both players the same public board and banks. On the first outer-column activation, generate one rank-2 Wands Card for each eligible outer column, with Supply 2 and Mana 0. Spend A's C1 instance as Supply, stage the Castle's destination at its allied Pawn party, agree the public plans, and resolve the merge. Both independently calculated states should contain the same nine original members, remaining Supply 1 and no duplicate instance. Reopen that accepted sequence from stored records. [REQ03 Appendix A]

3 Mechanical contract and golden fixtures
Authority and the meaning of a passing build
This chapter is an implementation aid for Hapa Versus Master Requirements v3 and its Design Companion v1. It preserves their three levels of instruction: selected product behavior, configurable capabilities, and concrete engineering fixtures. Public information and the three exclusive Minor Arcana uses are selected behavior. Alternative board sizes and damage policies are required capabilities. Temporary health values, numeric scales and the profile hapa_open_10x9_v3_01 make reproducible experiments possible; they are not permanent balance decisions. The Design Companion supplies presentation proposals without changing those rules. [REQ03; DES01]
A build passes by producing the specified state changes and explaining them accurately to both players. An attractive scene alone does not establish correct movement, and a correct rules engine alone does not establish understandable play. Keep a short drift ledger linking each change to its source passage, profile setting, expected result and evidence. A deliberate new experiment gets a new profile or content revision. It must not silently replace an existing fixture to make a test pass.
The tools below are recommendations grounded in current primary documentation. They have not been installed or run against a Hapa implementation for this reference. Pin versions compatible with the fork's actual Node, Vite, browser and packaging environment; the existence of a newer release is not evidence that it fits the project.

Board alternatives. Nine playable columns and nine, ten or eleven playable rows. Two side Enchantment columns plus two bank rows at each end produce full frames 11 × 13, 11 × 14 and 11 × 15. Each player has nine Major slots, nine Minor slots and N row Enchantment slots.
Vertical space. Six occupied bands: −3, −2, −1, +1, +2, +3. Zero is the original surface boundary. Grounded, flying, supported and exposed are different properties; a hilltop and flyer can share a band.
Physical geometry. Starting fixture: member square width 1, party body width 3, intervening strip width 1, center pitch 4. Territory centers are (4C−2, 4R−2). At an exact territory boundary, retain the prior territory until strict crossing. Four cardinal formation facings; diagonal travel preserves facing.
Opening army. Nine four-member Mount parties and nine five-member Pawn parties per seat: 81 members and 18 anchors each, 162 members and 36 anchors overall. Pawn slots 1, 2, 3 contain Swordsmen and 7, 9 Archers; its empty center still anchors income. Mount slots 5, 4, 6, 8 contain center, Pike, Mage, Priest. Banks, Supply, Mana and destinations start empty.
Clock. Repeating activation sequence: C1/C9, C2/C8, C3/C7, C4/C6, C5, C4/C6, C3/C7, C2/C8, C1/C9. Ticks 1–4 are the prologue; Round 1 is Ticks 5–13 and Round 2 starts at 14. Outer activations at Ticks 9 and 10 are intentional. A fully qualifying Round mints 17 ordinary cards per player, not 17 storage slots.
Orders. Active columns normally permit new Move, Formation, Skill and Spell orders. Existing movement, combat and work continue off-lane. Supply delivery can target any owned party in any state through the agreed action procedure. No gameplay order quota is added. One current destination replaces the previous legal destination; an invalid order leaves it intact.
Territory. Compare exact friendly and enemy center positions from the owner's home edge. The nearest qualifying enemy blocks; only a friendly center strictly before it qualifies as a frontier. Equal positions exclude that friendly center. Exposed, supported ground qualifies; flying or roofed occupants do not. Retainers alone cannot extend a column's frontier.
Public bank. One stored Minor Arcana per owned column. Ordinary minting replaces the old occupant in that slot atomically before spending. A column without a qualifying frontier does not mint and retains its old stored card. Ordinary pair activation permits two mints per player; center permits one per player.
Resource branches. Consume one instance for Supply, OR burn it for its Mana payload, OR use its attached ability. No branch also grants the other branches. Definitions survive instance consumption. Loaded Supply persists; temporary Mana expires each Tick. Stored cards do not share that expiry. Different columns can save enough cards to fund a large friendly-origin payment; there is no hard invasion gate.
Movement. Orthogonal and diagonal territory steps each cost one Supply, charged for actual fractional progress. Knight long-axis-first L travel costs three steps and fights along its route. Starting Speed is 2 for Knights, 1 for other mounts; unmounted Pawns lack ordinary travel. A new orthogonal direction normally costs one Tick to turn. The predeclared Knight bend costs no extra turn in this fixture.
Combat and victory. First contact exchanges immediately, with at most one ordinary exchange per engagement per Tick. Simultaneous damage, separate hits, Physical versus Magic channels, and defender-relative faces remain distinct. A live enemy anchor entering the fixed Capital at any occupied height or movement mode can win in the initial fixture; income eligibility is not the capture test. Commander death also loses. Same-batch mutual losses draw; earlier terminal results stop later simulation.
Keep the four damage demonstrations independently selectable: individual protection, line protection with attacker allocation, line protection with defender allocation, and party health maintenance. Do not implement a hybrid accidentally. A starting center has 6 HP, Physical Attack 3, frontal Physical Defense 1, and Magic Attack/Defense 0; ordinary retainers have 3 HP. Party health mode reserves 6 for a live center and maintains retainers at 3 each, removing excess retainers front, side, then rear. The interface must show its actual shared pool rather than invent member HP. [REQ03 chapters 6–9; Appendix A]

In one column A has anchors at global R2 and R7; B has R5 and R9. Each side's eligible frontier is its own home-relative R2. Repeat with the nearest blocker flying, on an exposed pit floor, and in a roofed tunnel. Only qualifying exposed-ground anchors block. Cutaway visibility alone changes none of these results.
Replacement and exclusive use
An occupied C1 bank slot is replaced at its next eligible mint; the old identity cannot be spent afterward. A no-mint C1 retains its old card. Trying to load and burn the same instance fails without a second grant. Loading a C1 card into an inactive-column party is allowed through the accepted action phase.
Pip provenance and expiry
Give a card three Red pips from friendly rows and one Green from enemy R6. Row Mana Double on R6 yields three Red plus two Green at a subsequent mint, not six Red plus two Green. Preserve each pip's origin. Burned unused Mana disappears at the Tick boundary; saved cards and loaded Supply remain.
Cross-column payment
C1 can store three Red while C5 stores three Rainbow, all from friendly land. Burn both and pay a four-Red cost with three Red plus one Rainbow, leaving two Rainbow in the pool. Replacing only one slot does not invalidate the other card or create a hidden enemy-territory requirement. Ordinary colors cannot pay a Rainbow-only cost.
Contact and arithmetic
With the Appendix A bodies and approach paths, the Knight/Queen diagnostic first touches at 125 of 1,000 quanta, one eighth Tick. Preserve partial-flank exposure. Attack 3 multiplied by one half, less Defense 1, causes 0.5 HP: 500,000 health units. Do not round the attack prematurely to 1.
Retreat and health
Resolve a scheduled same-time exchange before withdrawal casualties. The withdrawing party loses its own living members actually touching enemies; borrowed helpers return without being resurrected. In the maintenance diagnostic, a 24-HP party takes six one-point hits, leaving 18; after the center reserve, four retainers can remain, so two are removed without a second damage debit.
Terrain work
terrain_work_v3_01 begins after completed Tick 8 with an accessible worker at −3. Extract once at Tick 9, craft at Tick 10, then equip at Tick 18. Result: exhausted deposit, the Metal consumed, one armor instance, worker frontal Physical Defense 2. Replay or filling the cavity must not duplicate the deposit, Metal or armor.
Cards and response
Ember Strike consumes its card plus two Red from other resources; a friendly party in an active column targets one living enemy member in any of the eight neighboring territories at the same height, with no line-of-sight restriction, for one two-point Magic Front hit. Blue Denial consumes its own card plus two Blue, can respond from an inactive column, and counters a pending counterable Spell. Countering does not refund costs.

Also preserve the ordinary formation restriction during contact: a one-Tick rearrangement uses the old combat arrangement until successful completion. Wheel Formation changes duration, not permission to escape contact. Rechecking an invalid completion retains the old arrangement and emits failure. Merge, rearrangement and helper movement preserve attack markers; they cannot produce extra ordinary volleys by changing identities. [REQ03 chapters 6–10; Appendix A]
4 Public agreement deterministic state and replay
Public agreement is part of the test surface
The initial public protocol has no concealed plan and no invented Unready button. A first Ready record names the Tick, phase, prior state hash and both current plan revisions and hashes. It freezes that sender's own plan. The other player can still edit; that makes the earlier acknowledgment stale. The frozen player must acknowledge the updated pair without editing their own plan. Resolution begins only when both valid acknowledgments name the same pair. Duplicate delivery does nothing. Packet arrival never determines action priority. Ordinary queues preserve each player's order and alternate, with A first on odd global Ticks and B first on even ones. [REQ03 chapters 10, 14; Appendix B]
Exercise that sequence in two clients, including a delayed earlier revision, duplicate Ready, reconnect, and an invalid command after a valid one. Disconnect is interruption or pause, not concession. An automated test's Tick budget produces an unfinished experiment, not a competitive draw. A response window pauses simulation. Under public_stack_v1 the opponent receives first response to an ordinary Spell; two consecutive passes resolve the most recent pending action. A new response resets the passes. If actions remain after resolution, the Tick's first-action player receives the next opportunity. No animation timer decides that opportunity.
Separate the rules from their presentation
Keep a deterministic core that accepts a complete state, profile and accepted input, and returns a new state plus identified events. Transport carries authenticated records; the public protocol assembles the agreed input order; a controller submits choices; the view consumes state and events; history projects them into durable records. Camera, audio, frames per second and DOM focus do not enter rule calculations. A view may interpolate an authoritative path, but an animation callback cannot spend Supply or deal damage. [REQ03 chapters 14–16; DES01 chapter 15]
For the declared fixture, use integer scales of 1,000,000 per member width, Supply point and HP, with 1,000 quanta per Tick. Speed 1 advances 4,000 position units and spends 1,000 Supply units per quantum on a straight ordinary path. Preserve declared fractional remainders. Use swept geometry and simultaneous event batches; do not sample only final positions. Checked arithmetic must reject overflow rather than letting JavaScript silently lose integer precision.
Recommend one checked-in JSON Schema family, draft 2020-12, validated with Ajv's appropriate 2020 class. It makes manifests, profiles, commands, events and Card definitions explicit JSON contracts usable beyond TypeScript. Keep validation non-mutating: no coercion, removal of unknown fields or insertion of defaults into received signed records. Expand agreed defaults before hashing and signing. Strict schema validation catches malformed shapes; separate semantic validation checks ownership, phase, costs, references and installed operations. Ajv can generate standalone validators during the build, avoiding runtime schema compilation where the package environment requires it. [IC04]
Zod is a credible alternative for TypeScript-centric local forms. Its JSON Schema export cannot faithfully represent every transform or custom type, so it should not become a second competing protocol authority. The recommendation here is Ajv for the shared contract, not a claim that Zod cannot validate data. Uploaded Cards remain constrained definitions using installed operations; neither validator turns arbitrary JavaScript from a Card into an acceptable rules extension. [IC05]
Replay and canonical integrity
Appendix B's encoding is stricter than ordinary JSON acceptance. Reject duplicate keys during decoding, negative zero, unsafe integers, sparse arrays, non-finite numbers and unpaired surrogates. A schema applied after ordinary parsing cannot recover duplicate keys already discarded. Canonical objects sort keys by UTF-16 order; arrays retain order; strings use the declared escaping without Unicode normalization. Emit sorted keys directly: rebuilding a JavaScript object can reorder integer-looking keys. Hash UTF-8 bytes without a byte-order mark or whitespace. [REQ03 Appendix B]
Golden vector: canonical {"a":1,"b":2} produces sha256:43258cff783fe7036d8a43033f830adfc60ec037382473548ac742b888292777. The authoritative vector in Appendix B is the source of truth; verify every byte when incorporating this fixture.
Replay must load the original manifest, rules, content and numeric profile, then recompute accepted commands and recorded choices. Compare resulting events and checkpoints rather than trusting recorded damage. Include positions, remainders, terrain, inventory, durations, pending choices and any rules random stream in the state hash. Exclude camera, audio, wall-clock timing and derived presentation caches. Plans being edited are protocol inputs, not mutations of the prior authoritative game state. History projections and presentation events must be idempotent by event identity.
The initial fixture needs no gameplay randomness. If an experiment activates the specified xorshift32_v1 stream, preserve its nonzero seed and state; it is not a source for signing keys. Verify the complete identity and signature envelope as specified, including Ed25519 domain separation, rather than treating a matching state hash as proof of the sender's identity.
5 Lumina and rendering references
Read these recommendations against the two design authorities
The Master Requirements v3 owns gameplay and protocol behavior. The Design Companion v1 owns the proposed presentation, interaction and art direction. This reference chapter selects supporting implementation materials; it does not replace either document. The inspected uploads match the completed source snapshots identified in the source register. The most relevant sections are v3 Chapters 9 and 15–18, Appendix B, and Companion Chapters 3, 4 and 15. [REQ03, DES01]
The resulting architecture is deliberately small: one shared rules module calculates outcomes; Lumina presents those outcomes; each participant owns a signed Hypercore and independently validates the same public match; local adapters supply content and identity fixtures until the corresponding Hapa interfaces are connected. Six occupied height bands, excavatable solid and empty space, roofs, support surfaces, member formations and public plan revisions are application concepts that no selected library supplies automatically.
“Adopt” below means select for the implementation path, with pinned dependencies and an integration check. “Evaluate” means introduce only to answer a demonstrated need. “Reference only” means study the design without importing its architecture. Three js references for a truthful volume and readable view
Start with the official voxel-geometry manual. It explains why independent cube objects become expensive, generates faces where filled cells border empty cells, partitions the world into cells, and refreshes affected geometry after a change. This is a close conceptual match to Hapa's discrete excavation. Use the example to learn the meshing boundary, then implement that boundary against Hapa's own terrain records. Its terrain generation, coordinate defaults and visual style are not requirements. [ENG03]
Hapa should own a small authoritative volume model describing solid material, empty space, supporting floors, intact roofs, explicit traversal connections and deposits. Preserve zero as the original ground boundary, with three occupied bands above and three below. Territory bodies, one-member cubes and the gaps between territories are different scales. The renderer should consume a resolved edit and rebuild affected faces plus neighboring boundaries. It must not decide whether digging is legal, expose a deposit twice, or infer that a flying center blocks ground supply. Those decisions remain in the shared rules. [REQ03, DES01]
Begin with straightforward exposed-face generation grouped by material and manageable region. Keep stable cell/surface identifiers and a mapping from rendered faces back to those identifiers. This is enough to build the decisive demonstration: intact surface, open shaft, lateral tunnel with a roof, ground occupant above it, tunnel occupant and a flyer. A mesh can be rebuilt asynchronously for responsiveness, but interaction should use the current authoritative geometry and a clear pending visual until the replacement mesh is ready.
Greedy meshing is an optimization that merges neighboring compatible faces into larger rectangles. Binary Greedy Meshing v2 demonstrates occupancy masks, hidden-face culling and face merging in C++, with its own vertex-pulling renderer. Its MIT license was read at the pinned commit. Study the algorithm if measured draw or rebuild cost warrants it; do not port its 64-bit layout mechanically into JavaScript or quote its CPU benchmark as Hapa performance. Its inspected last commit is 30 March 2025, which is concrete history, not a claim of abandonment or ongoing support. [ENG09]
For Hapa, a merge must preserve material treatment and any boundary needed for inspection. A large rectangle can still carry a texture and a separate territorial marker; it cannot erase the logical distinction between two cells. Smooth isosurface extraction is not the initial solution: rounding a tunnel mouth or changing a support boundary would make the drawing less faithful to the specified cubes. This is a recommendation based on Hapa's geometry, not a judgment against smooth-terrain tools generally.
Use Three.js Raycaster as the initial pointer-query tool. Its layer filter, camera-aware rays and sorted intersections support an inspection adapter, but Hapa still needs explicit candidate selection. A pointer hit should resolve to a member, party anchor, terrain face or bank slot with stable identity and occupied band. When several public objects share a horizontal location, offer a target list and layer controls instead of silently selecting whichever triangle happens to be closest. The shared validator determines whether the selected target is legal. [ENG04; DES01]
Read the Material clipping documentation and the clipping-stencil example for view-only cuts. Local clipping needs renderer support; shadow clipping is separately configurable. Lumina's current renderer explicitly disables stencil, so the stencil example cannot simply be pasted into its current pipeline. Prefer an explicit cell/surface cutaway and visible cut edges first; evaluate stencil caps only if they solve a concrete presentation problem. In either implementation, retain the logical roof for attacks, support and supply eligibility, and make picking aware of the inspection view. [ENG05; ENG01]
The official transparency guide documents sorting tradeoffs and cutout alpha testing. It reinforces the Companion's recommendation to keep ordinary bodies and the selected floor opaque, using restrained ghosts or dither for obstruction. The color-management guide distinguishes color textures from data textures and explains output conversion with postprocessing. Keep painted color in the declared color space and masks as data; otherwise Hapa's suit/color vocabulary can drift between the card UI and the battlefield. These sources support rendering choices, not new gameplay visibility rules. [ENG06, ENG07]
When three mesh bvh earns its place
three-mesh-bvh builds spatial acceleration structures for Three.js geometry. The inspected repository is 0.9.18 at 0375ada0d674af3a4a169c4ad890239cf3d60cda, with MIT LICENSE and a commit dated 19 November 2026. Its declared Three.js peer range includes the pinned Lumina version, but a matching range is not a tested integration. [ENG08]
Evaluate it if a representative Hapa scene shows pointer queries or detailed mesh inspection consuming material time. Compare the same target sequence with the ordinary raycaster and the BVH adapter. The README and API describe direct/local-space queries, optional indirect indexing, refitting and worker construction. Excavation changes topology, so rebuilding the affected region's tree is the appropriate starting path; refitting unchanged triangle topology is a different operation. Preserve face-to-cell lookup if index ordering changes. Worker generation can temporarily transfer geometry buffers, so do not detach the currently displayed mesh without a replacement strategy. [ENG08]
Keep this acceleration cache out of authoritative state and replays. Hapa's fixed-point body sweeps, contact arbitration, facing, six incoming faces and support rules require the specified outcome calculations. A fast floating-point mesh query does not automatically implement them. The success criterion is faster equivalent inspection with unchanged legal selections and state hashes, not the presence of another dependency.
6 Peer records and application packaging
Two real peer logs with a small explicit agreement protocol
Hypercore is the selected durable log primitive. Current primary documentation describes directory-backed cores, appending blocks, verified reads and replication; it also exposes truncation and forks. The actual inspected repository package is 11.40.4, despite an older sentence in its README referring to Hypercore 10 as latest. Use the pinned package and API evidence, not that stale summary sentence. Hapa's accepted match prefix must remain protected at the application level. [ENG10]
Create two independent participants with separate identity/session keys and storage roots. On one development machine, peer A's writable log and peer B's writable log still belong to separate processes or independently initialized peers. Each retains a read copy of the other's log. A launcher can start them, but cannot accept a command on their behalf or choose the winning state. Core construction, ready(), append(), get() and replicate() are the primitive operations to study; the application supplies the manifest, signed message envelope and transition validator. [ENG10; REQ03]
Start from the existing v3 public-planning contract. Plan revisions are visible. First Ready freezes the author's own plan; it names the prior-state hash and the exact pair of plan revisions reviewed. If the other participant revises its still-editable plan, the earlier acknowledgment becomes stale and requires acknowledgment of the new pair. Only matching Ready records permit resolution. Responses follow the rule-supplied priority token, never arrival order. Both clients independently run the transition and record receipts naming the resulting state fingerprint. Do not substitute a generic last-writer-wins merge, a host decision or mandatory commit–reveal. [REQ03]
Hyperswarm supplies peer discovery by a 32-byte topic and encrypted connection streams; it also provides connection to a known peer key. Its client/server option names describe finding or accepting connections, not a privileged game referee. Bind the received network identity to the manifest's intended participant rather than trusting membership in a discovery topic. HyperDHT permits configured bootstrap nodes and documents the infrastructure needed for a private network. This supports replaceable discovery infrastructure; it does not establish that every network will connect without forwarding or that discovery is itself match authority. [ENG11, ENG12]
Keep four identities separate in code and records: the participant's DID, its application-signing verification key, its Hypercore log identifier and its network connection key. The identity adapter binds them for the match. A local fixture identity uses real signing keys and independent storage but must be labeled development identity, not verified Hapa identity. For integrated play, retain v3's DID-associated public match-intention record with the new match-log keys and deck fingerprints. The reference stack does not supply that Hapa service contract. [REQ03]
hypercore-crypto exposes Ed25519 keyPair(), sign() and verify(). Its data() operation hashes an internal Merkle leaf; it is not the SHA-256 operation specified for Hapa application fingerprints. Use an established SHA-256 implementation through a small runtime adapter, with v3's canonical integer-only JSON, signature prefix and frozen vectors unchanged. A valid signature establishes authorization of bytes; the rules validator still decides legality. Do not expose application secret keys or storage handles to creator animation descriptors. [ENG13; REQ03]
For recovery, store accepted records, receipts, the manifest, pinned definitions and a checkpoint. Reconnect by fetching missing log records and replaying from an agreed prefix. A changed accepted fork must be detected rather than silently replacing the past. An unfinished session remains a resumable or exportable record; final forfeit or reward policy does not block this engineering work. Export held bytes for offline replay, since a remote identifier alone does not guarantee that another peer still serves the content. [REQ03]
Protomux is a modest optional helper for a separate handshake/content channel on an existing framed stream. Its actual MIT license and APIs were inspected. Use protocol and match identifiers, explicit encodings and bounded messages. Durable Plan, Ready, Choice and Receipt records remain in the logs; a transient channel must not become an unrecorded alternate path for outcomes. For the first bundled fixture, pre-agreed local content can avoid adding a second application protocol until it is useful. [ENG14; REQ03]

Its runtime explanation places networking/storage in a Bare worker and communicates with the UI through IPC. The inspected pear-runtime 1.4.0 repository carries Apache-2.0. Evaluate this as a packaging seam around Lumina, with the same shared rules and versioned data contracts; do not rewrite the game into a new UI framework. Corestore's package declares MIT, while its complete inspected tree likewise has no such file. Colyseus' current state documentation explicitly puts state mutation on the server, with clients sending requests and receiving synchronized changes. Its repository carries MIT, and the inspected commit is dated 18 November 2026. It is a credible reference for room lifecycles and update ergonomics, but adopting that authority would change Hapa's decided protocol. 8 Art and interface production resources
Purpose and recommended production path
Hapa needs a repeatable way to turn an approved Card identity into readable characters, terrain, effects and interface elements. The difficult work is consistency: a shield must stay on the same arm during orbit; a spell must indicate its real target; a roofed tunnel must remain selectable; and an imported asset must retain its creator and source history. A larger collection of beautiful images does not solve those requirements by itself.
The proposed baseline is Krita for brushed-pixel source cleanup, an optional licensed Aseprite installation or evaluated LibreSprite build for frame animation, and small Pillow-based scripts for fixed-grid export and validation. Blender is useful when a controlled three-dimensional proxy can maintain anatomy and equipment across views. TexturePacker becomes useful if measured atlas needs justify a richer importer and the appropriate license. No recommendation here authorizes a purchase or asserts that any of these tools is already installed in the Hapa fork.
“Adopt” means a recommended project dependency or review reference after ordinary version and environment checks. “Evaluate” means a bounded proof before committing the pipeline. “Reference only” means learn a design principle without importing another game's assets. These are companion recommendations, not changes to v3 gameplay. Sources and license statements were checked on 22 November 2026 UTC; the accompanying source register preserves exact links and claim scopes.
Eleven resources with specific jobs
Aseprite for an artist friendly animation source
Evaluate — animation authoring and reproducible export. Aseprite's official CLI supports background batch processing and export of a sprite sheet plus JSON. Its documented example is a starting point for a version-pinned export wrapper: [AR01]
aseprite -b animation.ase --sheet sheet.png --data sheet.json
The wrapper must translate exported metadata into Hapa's manifest; Aseprite JSON is not automatically a Lumina SpriteSheet.
Aseprite is source-available under its current EULA, not a blanket GPL dependency. Its FAQ permits commercial artwork and distinguishes individual use from company licenses per developer. Keep a licensed executable outside the game's asset package. Record the exporter version and source file, and confirm the applicable terms before installing it on shared infrastructure. The editor's license does not grant rights to imported references or purchased brushes.
Use the proof to finish one Swordsman idle, walk and attack set, then inspect two opposed views at tactical size. Adopt only if source editing, naming and unattended export are dependable. Avoid building the whole workflow around a manual export dialog.
LibreSprite for a separately tested open source alternative
Evaluate — pixel cleanup and frame animation without assuming Aseprite parity. The official LibreSprite repository identifies it as a fork of the last GPLv2 Aseprite version and licenses the application under GPL-2.0. That makes it a meaningful alternative to test, not a drop-in guarantee for current Aseprite scripts, file features or CLI switches. [AR02]
Evaluate the exact released build against the same source asset and output contract: dimensions, alpha, frame order, animation timing and retained editable layers. Use its own documented capabilities rather than copying commands from modern Aseprite. Record any conversions that discard information. If the project distributes the program or modifications, retain its applicable notices and source obligations; that is separate from the licensing of the images being edited.
The practical decision is whether the tested tool helps the artist produce a correct rendition. Hapa does not need to support two editor-specific production systems simply to offer a nominal choice.
Krita for the brushed pixel source of truth
Adopt — layered source painting, cleanup and controlled flat export. Krita's documented CLI can export a source document with krita approved.kra --export --export-filename approved.png. This is useful for repeatable KRA-to-PNG conversion; it is not evidence that every animation-editing operation has a complete headless API. [AR03]
Krita's license page distinguishes the GPLv3 application from the artwork it produces and explicitly allows commercial art. The same page states that distributed Krita plugins using its extension API must be shared under the GPL. A standalone file-conversion wrapper and a distributed in-process plugin therefore need separate licensing treatment.
For Hapa, keep the higher-resolution approved painting and its intentional delivery-size cleanup. Use broad brush clusters, selective ink and stable material planes. Do not obtain the final style by shrinking a detailed painting and applying a global pixel filter. Preserve gear, silhouette and foot-anchor guides as editable layers; exported layers should have stable names and a documented compositing order.
Blender for controlled view capture
Evaluate — consistent eight-view proxies and repeatable captures. Blender supports background rendering and Python scripts, allowing an agent-authored capture job to set cameras, poses, output paths and frame ranges. Its documentation stresses command-line argument order. Blender is GPL software; its application license does not impose the GPL on rendered artwork. Imported meshes, textures, rigs and add-ons still retain their own terms. [AR04]
Build one simple, anatomically consistent character proxy with shield and weapon attached to the correct body sides. Capture at eight azimuths, using the companion's proposed 50-degree downward view as the initial art trial. A capture can use an orthographic camera for consistent scale, but that is an authoring choice: Hapa's runtime rig is perspective, so the output must be checked in that rig. It does not add an orthographic gameplay mode.
Use the captures as painting guides or approved rendition sources. Blender is not required to replace the game's renderer, and a rigid proxy is not a substitute for the desired brushed-pixel finish. Pin camera settings, lighting, rig version and pose names so a repaired frame can be reproduced.
TexturePacker for later atlas optimization
Evaluate — measured packing needs, with the correct commercial or CI license. The official documentation supports CLI builds and reusable packing projects. At this review date, TexturePacker Essential is free only for non-commercial projects; commercial use requires a license. The vendor separately lists desktop and Docker/CI licensing. Do not describe Essential as a free commercial build dependency. [AR05]
More efficient packing is not automatically compatible with Lumina. The current SpriteSheet addresses equal-size cells through column and row coordinates. A rotated, trimmed or irregularly packed region needs an importer that respects source dimensions, offsets, rotation and foot anchor, or a repack into fixed cells. Without that work, apparent memory savings can produce jumping feet, clipped weapons or the wrong frame.
Begin with the simpler fixed-grid pipeline below. Revisit TexturePacker when real texture-memory or packaging measurements identify a benefit. Check the chosen features and build-machine license against the current vendor terms; store project settings and tool versions, never activation secrets, alongside the build recipe.
Pillow for a small inspectable asset pipeline
Adopt — fixed-grid composition, metadata checks and review sheets. Pillow's official API provides image loading, RGBA manipulation and PNG output. Its current documentation identifies the license as MIT-CMU. Hapa's proposed atlas packer would be project code built with those primitives; it is not an existing Pillow feature named “Hapa export.” [AR06]
Use a fixed canvas for each frame, preserve the shared anchor and place frames into deterministic cells. Produce both an atlas and a contact sheet with view/action/frame labels. Inspect dimensions and decoded memory before accepting a file; retain Pillow's image-size protections. Hash source and output bytes, and verify that all frame coordinates lie within the atlas.
This keeps the initial importer small and reviewable. A fixed grid trades some unused transparent area for simple addressing and consistent anchors. Measure that trade before replacing it with a more complex packing scheme. An MIT-CMU tool dependency does not make the input artwork or exported atlas public domain.
Kenney for disposable prototype components
Adopt selectively — prototype icons, calibration textures and temporary interface pieces. Kenney's official support page identifies assets on its asset pages as CC0 and permits commercial use without required attribution. The Development Essentials page supplies a concrete CC0 texture pack useful for diagnostic surfaces. The site's branding and other material should not be swept into that asset-license statement. [AR07]
Use temporary pieces to prove selection, scale, borders and controls while Hapa-specific art is being made. Mark them as prototype dependencies in the manifest and keep their asset-page URL and license file. They should not silently become the final Hapa identity or substitute generic icons for the game's distinct suit, alignment, owner and damage-channel meanings.
A pack-level license check remains preferable to a directory called “free assets.” Recording attribution voluntarily also makes later replacement and auditing easier, even when the asset license does not demand it.
Poly Haven for material and lighting source material
Evaluate — selected textures, models or HDRIs as source material. Poly Haven licenses its downloadable assets under CC0. Its API is a separate matter: the current official API page permits free commercial access, while the API terms require a recognizable client identity and visible source credit for a live integration. Those API conditions do not add attribution requirements to downloaded or self-hosted CC0 assets. [AR08]
For Hapa, sample a few relevant stone, earth, wood or lighting references, then make intentional brushed material renditions at the game's scale. Photorealistic texture detail and realistic HDRI contrast can overwhelm small tactical characters. Test material tiling, luminance and orbit shimmer before building a library.
Prefer a curated, cached project set with asset IDs, source files, hashes and license records. There is no need to depend on a live asset service during a match. If an authoring browser later uses the live API, implement its current attribution and identification requirements explicitly; the API code's AGPL license is also separate from the downloaded assets' CC0 status.
Three js and the pinned Lumina sprite contract
Adopt — engineering references for exports and runtime validation. Three.js documents distinct magnification/minification filtering, texture color spaces and transparency limitations. Color images normally need sRGB interpretation; masks and other data use the appropriate non-color treatment. Nearest magnification can preserve deliberate pixel edges, while minification and mipmaps require a separate material decision. Overlapping transparent surfaces can expose depth-ordering problems. [AR09]
The inspected Lumina revision uses equal-cell frame coordinates, four camera-relative directions and a mirror fallback. Eight authored views require a selector extension; a finished asymmetric sprite must not pass through an uncontrolled mirror fallback. The combat shader also uses partially transparent alpha, including 204, for glow. Ordinary soft brush edges can therefore acquire unintended emission.
Declare a material profile in each rendition. A cutout combat sprite follows the chosen binary-alpha/reserved-glow contract; a deliberately translucent VFX asset uses its separately implemented path. Do not reject or normalize all semi-transparent art with one global rule. Recheck color, anchors, filtering and alpha in the actual renderer rather than approving the PNG in an image viewer alone.
Riot s clarity and VFX practice
Reference only — visual hierarchy and precise effect boundaries. Riot's clarity article explains why silhouettes, readable abilities and restrained visual noise matter. Its VFX update account prioritizes incorrect or missing indicators and carries corrected bounds across cosmetic variants. These are useful production principles, not permission to reuse Riot's characters, effects, interface art or code. [AR10]
Hapa's application is concrete: preserve the meaningful footprint across every rendition; make source, target and incoming face recoverable; give decisive events more emphasis than ambient glows; and distinguish pending from resolved effects. A more expensive-looking Card rendition must not obscure an enemy's protection or alter the apparent legal area.
Review criticism in operational terms: “the overhead arrows flash the Front shield,” “the trail implies a wider area,” or “the pending Spell appears to land before Pass.” Each comment identifies a repairable mismatch. General requests for spectacle come after those mismatches are fixed.
Xbox Accessibility Guidelines for acceptance work
Adopt as a review rubric — not an accessibility certification. XAG 103 recommends additional sensory channels and alternatives to color-only communication. XAG 117 addresses moving content and camera effects; XAG 118 calls for photosensitivity testing. The wider guideline set also provides text, focus, navigation, audio and narration references. [AR11]
For Hapa, pair alignment color with a named emblem; provide captions and readable event records; retain keyboard-accessible target lists; and give response controls a clear focus order. Different visual shapes improve color independence but do not, by themselves, make critical information accessible to a blind player. Meaningful narration or other suitable access needs deliberate implementation.
Offer reduced motion and effects while preserving exact source, boundary and result. Test the combined scene: several individually mild spells can create excessive flashing or hide labels together. Check real composited backgrounds and enlarged text, and test with representative users. A good palette, grayscale screenshot or absence of intentional strobe effects cannot certify the complete experience.
9 From a Card identity to a runtime rendition
From an approved identity to a validated rendition
Establish the asset contract before generating variants
Start with a stable Hapa identity, gameplay role, approved references and a presentation-only asset ID. Record anatomical left and right, major silhouette features, clothing materials, equipment attachments and the member's foot anchor. A source Card's executable rules remain a separate versioned definition. A costume does not grant armor, and an animated spear does not determine collision range.
The companion proposes a 128×128 frame trial with roughly 64 authored texels per displayed member width. Keep that a production trial, not a change to physical member width. Reserve transparent space for weapons and casting poses so the anchor never moves to accommodate a drawing. Specify view naming and animation names before producing a large batch.
Generate or paint source material with complete lineage
Generate one controlled proof view, or paint it manually, then check identity and tactical readability. Preserve the original output even after cleanup. Record inspiration references, exact prompt history, prompt author, generator/version/settings when available, image artifacts, edit parent and artist. An unknown field is labeled unknown; it is not filled with invented authorship.
Use generated turnarounds as candidates requiring review. Do not promote a montage with plausible-looking labels directly into an atlas. Generated characters can change costume details, hands or proportions between views. Repair a failed view before using it as the reference for further images. Imported licensed artwork needs its own usage record even when it appears inside an otherwise original painting.
Clean at delivery size and evaluate the extended views
Approve broad material planes, readable face/role, deliberate brush clusters and selective ink at tactical scale. Keep the existing four-view path available for the first proxy playable. After the proposed eight-view selector and art trial are approved, build its views at 45-degree camera-relative increments. Define zero as the view looking toward the actor's front and record increasing-angle convention explicitly. Test it with a shield-bearing model; ambiguous labels such as “left” are insufficient because anatomical and camera-left differ.
Eight art views do not add eight legal formation facings. The starting combat fixture has four cardinal facings. The renderer maps relative camera angle to the art while the rules keep their own facing. Use independent views for asymmetric gear, with mirror permission disabled unless the specific asset proves it is safe. Preview a complete orbit at ordinary and steep pitches, retaining normalized animation phase at every view transition.
Animate rule events without creating them
Use the companion's proposed clip set as an initial budget: idle, walk, weapon action, cast, hit reaction, fall and work, plus stable pending/interrupted poses. Declare per-clip frames, rate and loop flag. The proposed two-frame idle or six-frame attack is an authoring choice; it does not define Tick length or number of hits.
Drive impacts from recorded gameplay events. Six Above arrow hits need six recoverable calculations even if their trails overlap aesthetically. A response pause holds work progress and movement; decorative settling cannot imply that the Spell resolved. A charge effect ends at its recorded impact, and subsequent stationary attacks use ordinary combat presentation. Reduced-motion playback retains the same event identity and outcome.
Export a simple atlas and a complete manifest
First export independent RGBA frames with a common canvas, anchor and scale. Normalize them only under their declared material profile. Pack deterministic equal-size cells and emit animation frame coordinates matching Lumina's current contract. Keep trim and rotation disabled in this baseline. If a future importer supports more sophisticated packing, it must preserve original frame size and anchor through every transform.
The manifest includes source Card/identity, immutable asset version, content hash, file dimensions, cell dimensions, view convention, anchor, pixels-per-unit conversion, animation lists, timing, loop flags, mirror permissions, sockets, draw order, color-space declaration, alpha/glow profile and fallback. It also links original source files and lineage records. Packaging should not throw away the evidence that made the rendition trustworthy.
If rendition metadata enters a signed v3 canonical record, keep it integer-safe: integer pixel anchors and rational scale or rate fields, or a separately hashed art artifact under its own schema. Convert to renderer-native numeric values at the presentation boundary. Cosmetic floating-point settings must not weaken the integer-only gameplay encoding. [REQ03 Appendix B]
Validate inside the actual game
Check malformed or oversized images, missing views, duplicate or out-of-range frames, drifting anchors, unexpected alpha values and absent provenance. Then load the asset in a small renderer scene containing a floor, tunnel roof, flyer, bright/dark backgrounds and an opposing asymmetric member. Capture the actual tactical zoom, continuous orbit and an animation-view transition.
Finally use recorded semantic fixtures: six Above arrows, front sword protection, a public counter window, a corner helper returning home, a roofed tunnel target and a completed excavation. Confirm that the same state and events produce the same meaning under reduced effects, muted audio and enlarged interface text. Missing cosmetic art uses a labeled neutral fallback; missing executable behavior must remain a separate content error.

10 Wisdom from games and engineering references
These references supply design principles and implementation techniques. Apply each through a small Hapa fixture while preserving the selected rules. The comparisons are recommendations, not new balance decisions.
League and ARAM make concentrated conflict legible
Riot describes League's ARAM as randomly assigned champions fighting in a single lane, five players per side. Its clarity article emphasizes recognizable silhouettes, accurate ability presentation and visual emphasis proportional to gameplay importance. Current ARAM has map variations; Howling Abyss is not the only possible current setting. [WIS01, WIS02]
A contested Hapa frontage should reveal commitment, relevant protection and the next exchange's stakes. Emphasize decisive Spells over routine movement and keep their causes inspectable. A paired-column highlight identifies new-order permission; it must not hide combat continuing elsewhere. Do not import real-time MOBA pacing, reflex execution, random rosters, five-player teams or respawn assumptions. Hapa's public agreement and response pauses remain deliberate decision space. Test the exciting moment at reduced motion as well as full effects.
Chess offers a movement vocabulary not a complete combat model
FIDE chess uses alternating moves, piece-specific movement and checkmate. A move cannot expose or leave the mover's own king in check. Ordinary capture removes the opposing piece from its square; these rules do not describe a health-and-formation battle. [WIS03]
Familiar movement silhouettes help explain a Castle, Bishop or Queen's destinations. Show Hapa's differences at the point of action: travel persists, Supply pays for progress, speed affects arrival, bodies can meet between anchors and nine members retain their own roles. A Knight's diagnostic route is physical travel with possible contact, not a teleport over the intervening fight. Do not add chess check, checkmate, one-hit capture, promotion or alternating single-piece turns by association. Keep the King archetype, mobile Commander and fixed Capital visibly distinct; their names do not make them interchangeable victory objects.
Go teaches the value of connection and cutting
The British Go Association introduces territory as surrounded vacant points. A stone or orthogonally connected string has adjacent empty liberties; occupying all its liberties captures it. Its introductory scoring example counts territory and prisoners. These are specific Go mechanisms, not a universal definition of territorial control. [WIS04]
Ask which advance stays connected and which position becomes isolated. Explain a cutoff through the nearest eligible enemy and the friendly frontier before it. Do not calculate Hapa income by flood-filling enclosed regions, counting Go liberties or removing a party simply because it is surrounded. Exposure and support matter independently: a visible roofed occupant and a flyer do not become ground-income blockers. A good learning comparison uses the same column with an exposed enemy, a roofed enemy and no blocker, then shows the different eligible frontier.
Magic demonstrates precise costs timing and exceptions
Magic's Comprehensive Rules distinguish Mana, costs, priority and the stack. Successive passes resolve the top pending spell or ability; a response normally resolves before the action beneath it. Card text can override an applicable rule in its specified situation. The inspected rules are effective 6 November 2026. [WIS05]
For Hapa, name the source, payment, legal target, response window, result and duration. Separate an instance consumed as Supply, burned for Mana or used for its attached ability. Make a countered paid spell explain its spent cost. Do not copy Magic's five-color system, land-play cadence, turn phases, hidden hands or complete priority machinery. Hapa's named public response policy supplies its own order. Likewise, “Card text overrides rules” does not authorize arbitrary prose or creator JavaScript to execute: the versioned interpreter must support the declared operation, and the match must agree its definition.
Command objects separate intent from execution
Robert Nystrom's Command chapter turns an operation into a value that can be selected, queued, logged or replayed. The same interface can receive human or AI decisions. Serializable commands also help transport those decisions; undo is another possible use, not an automatic game rule. [WIS06]
Mouse, keyboard, bot and test should request the same typed action with actor, target, parameters and plan revision. Validation and the shared transition still decide legality; a command object is not permission to execute it. Replaying accepted commands and recorded choices should reconstruct the same result. Do not import arbitrary undo into a frozen public plan or accepted match history because the tutorial demonstrates it. An editable proposal, a replay cursor and a committed gameplay action have different responsibilities.
A star searches the graph that the game defines
Red Blob Games explains A* through graph nodes, weighted edges and a heuristic. The returned path says which nodes connect, not how an actor physically traverses the world. A heuristic that does not overestimate supports optimal paths under the represented costs. [WIS07]
Define the search objective: least Supply, earliest arrival and safest route differ. A graph needs the relevant surface, occupied band, support and explicit connections; two positions above and below one roof cannot collapse into the same node. Search can assist an allowed route preview or experiment, while swept bodies and the shared validator determine actual traversal and contact. Do not replace the specified straight routes or long-axis-first Knight fixture with an unconstrained shortest-path solver. Fix neighbor order and equal-cost tie handling when search results become agreed gameplay data.
Fixed stepping separates simulation from display speed
Glenn Fiedler's Fix Your Timestep explains how variable frame duration can alter a simulation, how bounded or fixed steps address that problem, and how an accumulator and interpolation separate simulation updates from rendering. It also discusses the overload problem when simulation cannot keep up. [WIS08]
Hapa's existing numerical profile supplies its Tick subdivisions and fixed-point arithmetic. Use those accepted rules to calculate motion and simultaneous events; use interpolation only to present the resolved trajectory. A slow renderer must not change who touches first, spend extra Supply or choose a different winner. Do not equate one Tick with one display frame or add a 60-Hz gameplay requirement because an article uses that example. Public choices pause simulation, so waiting time must not accumulate into later gameplay catch-up. Fixed stepping is one ingredient: deterministic arithmetic, event ordering, content versions and recorded choices still need their own verification.
A useful transfer check
For each borrowed idea, name the improvement and the Hapa rule it preserves. Keep a small inspectable destination, counter, cutoff or interception example as evidence, so the next agent inherits the principle accurately.

Decide the working brush density, silhouette rules and distant token threshold from those captures. Full eight-view production follows a successful selector and art trial; it does not delay the first proxy playable.
What exactly should the first territory-enhancement Card do? Earth Study in M05 demonstrates appearance and targeting, but it is not a priced playable definition. Select one concrete effect with a named operation, legal area, cost, duration, source, stacking behavior and expiry. Use an existing defined modifier where suitable, or author an explicit new versioned operation and fixture. Only then should artists encode its bonus through a fortification, movement cue or income marker. Until that selection, keep the enhancement labeled as a concept.
Which measured hardware and display profiles define the first supported experience? The source build passed, but there is no new Hapa runtime or performance benchmark. Record the first PC and Mac test configurations, viewport, device pixel ratio and quality settings. Measure the full opening roster with overlapping row, column, terrain and response effects, including reduced effects and enlarged text. Choose frame-time, memory and load budgets from those results. The existence of a powerful development computer does not establish a minimum supported machine.
Which signals must every collectible rendition preserve? The player should be able to identify a unit's role, owner, facing, source effect, incoming face and exact meaningful boundary even when an artist changes costume, materials or portrait treatment. Compare a base rendition and two expressive variants using the same recorded actions. Approve the shared signals and allowed variation, then record that contract in the asset manifest and review skill. Rarity and visual expense do not justify a larger apparent target area or a persistent aura that hides other state.
These questions carry forward the Companion's bounded decisions with evidence that a prototype can produce. They do not reopen the selected board economics, public information, separate Card branches or peer authority. An unresolved ecosystem API should use a clearly labeled adapter and fixture identity until the real interface is supplied; that adapter cannot claim verified Hapa issuance or entitlement.

A gameplay change belongs in the requirements and its fixtures. A presentation choice belongs in the Companion or a versioned presentation profile. A tool replacement or newer source belongs here. Update the linked artifacts together so that a future agent does not reconcile three different answers by guessing.

Missing cosmetic art uses a visible neutral fallback; unsupported gameplay operations remain explicit errors. Missing ecosystem APIs use labeled local adapters with the same contract. Browser or discovery failures preserve logs, exact commands and diagnostic evidence; continue with headless or local transport fixtures while clearly identifying what remains untested. Never replace independent agreement with a privileged server to obtain a green result. Escalate only a genuine contradiction that changes behavior; configurable values already supplied by the profile do not require another owner decision.
